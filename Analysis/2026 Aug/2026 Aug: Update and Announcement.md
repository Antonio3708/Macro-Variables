# August: Update and Announcement

For the last four months, I have stopped writing analyses.
The reason I stopped writing was that there was no new topic or insight that I found worth sharing.

Yes, the war was an event that I did not anticipate, and it could have been a new topic for an analysis. 
However, I found it pointless to write a new piece, as my view did not really change even with the introduction of a new variable.
In fact, despite the introduction of this new variable, most of the market behavior aligned with the view I had presented in my previous analysis.

* **In February, I warned that the market was in a fragile environment and that a high level of volatility/correction was likely in the near future.**
    * The market remained volatile for a period of time following February and eventually faced a correction after the outbreak of the Iran war.

* **I mentioned that semiconductors and other industries benefiting from increasing CapEx would show strong performance.**
    * Semiconductors such as Micron showed very strong performance, gaining approximately 170–200%.

* **I mentioned that gold and other commodities were not as attractive as before.**
    * Gold and silver subsequently showed weak performance.
  
* **I mentioned that QT was unlikely despite Warsh's stance.**
    * The Federal Reserve has not stopped its repo market operations.

Still, one prediction turned out to be wrong.

I mentioned that while the American market was in a fragile environment, the Korean market would face less severe consequences.
One of the reasons I made this prediction was the massive retail cash balance and strong sentiment among Korean retail investors. 
At the time, despite strong selling pressure from U.S.-based dealers, the Korean market showed resilience, backed by significant purchases from retail investors and domestic institutions.
To my understanding, Korea had strong support from retail investors, and there was no major event that could potentially flip their sentiment other than a financial crisis or a global supply-chain disruption. 
As I believed that the potential for war was low, I excluded the possibility of a global supply-chain disruption—which ultimately happened and flipped investor sentiment.

Of course, the Korean market bounced strongly soon afterward, and one of my biggest positions, Micron, also had a great run.

After the run, there was a change in my portfolio. 
To get straight to the point, I sold my Micron position. 
I did not share this change for a couple of reasons. 

First, I did not change my view on the semiconductor industry. 
My exit was a technical decision rather than an expression of disbelief in the industry in general.

Second, it was a short-term trade. 
I found it inappropriate to post about short-term trades since the focus of this GitHub is macro and industry research and analysis. 

Third, I did not believe that my decision to sell would pay off to this extent.
While I sold Micron based on the results from my private models and my general analysis of the market, the timing of the exit was extremely lucky; it happened to be only two days before the severe crash. 
I am not a wizard who can predict what will happen in the market. 
I expected a correction, but I did not expect it to happen only two days later, nor did I expect the correction to be this severe.

Lastly, I wanted to take more time to study rather than publish analyses. 
Since the outbreak of the war, I have worked on a couple of machine-learning projects and naturally exposed myself with more data. 
I believe that my knowledge is still limited and that I need to focus more on learning than writing with limited knowledge. 
For this very reason, I found it inappropriate to post anything related to short-term trading, where even very knowledgeable individuals have very little success.


After my exit, I began buying semiconductor stocks again, but semiconductors are no longer my largest position, nor do I have a plan to make them one.

Since my exit, many things have happened in the market, and these changes have been significant enough to alter my views on specific asset classes and industries.
One of the major changes is my perspective on gold, which I will address in the next post.
